# Plan 07a: the serial ports

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** UART1 and UART2 run as the spec says:
- RX by circular DMA with the receiver timeout, TX by DMA, hardware DE;
- frames cut transparently, or by Modbus RTU's t1.5/t3.5;
- a multi-instance `serial` module with subscribers, the bus mutex and transactions;
- the WebSocket console's UART modes.

Verified with two USB-RS485 adapters on the bench.

**Spec:** §6.1 (`serial_rx[n]` 26, `serial_hb[n]`), §6.4 (rules per port), §6.5, §5.3 (SRAM1: rings and TX buffers), §5.4 (USART rows), §9.1 (configs at boot), §10.1 (console UART modes). **Roadmap:** row 07 is split into 07a (this plan) and 07b (`iomgr`, Reload, ADC3, factory record).

## Rules that apply

- Configs are read once at boot (`port1`, `port2`, `system`). A change applies after a restart.
- Keep it simple; run only the affected tests; Codex reviews before the PR.
- The ESP32 `serial.c` is the behavioural baseline:
  - subscribers;
  - the bus mutex for writes, transactions and line changes;
  - a transaction owns the bytes of its reply; a frame from another slave goes to the subscribers;
  - the reply window after a transparent write;
  - 485 collision avoidance;
  - the heartbeat.

  RFC 2217 is gone (STM32 scope), so `serial_set_line` and `serial_purge` go too.

## Architecture

- **`components/serial/serial_frame.{h,c}`** is pure C, host-tested (`test_serial_frame`). Per port, it is the logic between the DMA ring and the frames:
  - **The reader:** the RX ring (2 KB; 4 KB for UART3 later) is consumed from the DMA's write position (`ring - NDTR`).
    - A lap counter from the HT/TC events tells when the writer has passed the reader. That case is counted as `overrun`, and the frame being built is dropped (spec §6.5).
  - **Transparent cut:** a frame ends at `packetLength` bytes, or at the receiver timeout. The timeout is programmed as the gap in bit times: `packetInterval` ms, or 3.5 characters, at least 2 ms.
  - **Modbus RTU cut** (a transaction in flight). The DMA gives no per-byte event, so this is how a byte in the t1.5–t3.5 window is seen (Codex r1):
    - RTOR is set to t1.5, so RTO fires only after t1.5 of silence;
    - at RTO the ISR records the DMA write position and arms the port's one-shot for t3.5 − t1.5;
    - **at expiry it is a violation** when the write position moved (a byte came in the window), or when the USART's `BUSY` flag is set (a character started before the deadline and is still arriving);
    - otherwise the frame is complete.
    - **A violation drops the frame and resynchronises:** every byte is ignored until a full t3.5 of silence, checked on each RTO by the same one-shot (position unchanged, `BUSY` clear). The tail is never taken as a new frame.
    - `serial_transact` gets "dropped: t1.5 violation" and counts it (Modbus Serial Line V1.02 §2.5.1.1). The board's own classification proves the gap: RTO says ≥ t1.5 of silence, and the byte came before t3.5.
    - Each RTO is cleared and the DMA runs on, so RX goes on after any number of timeouts (spec §6.5).

    Character times use the real framing (start + data + parity + stop). Above 19 200 baud, t1.5 = 750 µs and t3.5 = 1.75 ms.
  - **Line errors** (parity, framing, noise, USART overrun): the frame is dropped and counted.
  - **XON/XOFF** (`flowControl` 2) in software:
    - XOFF and XON from the device pause and resume our TX, and are taken out of the data;
    - we never send XOFF ourselves: the ring and `serial_rx` keep up (P2).
  - **The TX controller** (pure, host-tested; Codex r1):
    - a write is chunks of at most 2 KB;
    - **XOFF stops a chunk already being sent**, by clearing `CR3.DMAT`: the stream stops requesting, and what the USART holds finishes. XON sets it again and the chunk goes on;
    - each write has a deadline: the caller's timeout, plus about 2 character times per byte;
    - at the deadline, waiting for XON or for TC, and on a DMA transfer error, the write is aborted: stream disabled, `DMAT` cleared, the USART's TX drained, and an error returned;
    - every way out releases the bus mutex;
    - the next write starts clean (its XOFF state stays as the device last said).
  - **Timing helpers:** character time, RTO bits, t1.5 and t3.5, gap; the USART prescaler and BRR for 600 to 230 400 baud from the 120 MHz kernel clock (BRR ≤ 0xFFFF needs the prescaler at low rates).
- **`components/serial/uart_drv.c`** is the board side, per port: USART1 on PA9/PA10/PA12, USART2 on PD5/PD6/PD4, both AF7.
  - **Init:**
    - `HAL_UART_Init` with the config's word length, parity, stop bits and prescaler (its MspInit gives the pins, clocks and DMA handles from CubeMX);
    - `HAL_RS485Ex_Init` for hardware DE, with `DEAT`/`DEDT` of 1 bit time;
    - FIFO off;
    - **then our own LL interrupt handlers** for USART RTO, error flags and TC, and for the DMA streams. The HAL handler would treat RTOF as an error and abort RX (spec §6.5).
  - **RX:** DMA1 stream 2 (USART1) and 4 (USART2), circular, into a 2 KB ring in SRAM1. The HT and TC interrupts notify `serial_rx[n]`; the receiver timeout (`RTOEN`) and errors come through the USART interrupt.
  - **TX:** DMA1 stream 3 (USART1) and 5 (USART2) from a 2 KB buffer in SRAM1.
    - `serial_write` copies in chunks of at most 2 KB, refilling only after the stream's TC.
    - The caller's buffer can be anywhere (spec §5.4).
    - **DE is the USART's own** (hardware RS485 mode, DEM, on the `USARTx_DE` pin): the chip raises it before the start bit and drops it after the last stop bit. The write then waits for the USART's TC, not to drive DE, but so that the reply window and the next transaction start only after the last bit has left.
  - **One-shot timers for t3.5:** the compare channels CC1 and CC2 of TIM2, the free-running 1 MHz `now_us` counter, one per port. No new timer is used.
  - **NVIC:** USART1/2 and DMA1 S2–S5 at priority 6, TIM2 at 6. ISRs live in ITCM (spec §5.3).
- **`components/serial/serial.{h,c}`**, the ESP32 module made multi-instance (`port` 1 or 2 in every call):
  - one `serial_rx[n]` task per port, priority 26, 3 KB (spec §6.1), which drains the ring with bounded work per wake (P2);
  - `serial_subscribe`, `serial_write`, `serial_transact` (RTU cut during the transaction), counters, last activity;
  - `serial_hb[n]` (2 KB, priority 4) when the heartbeat is on.

  Its payload helper takes no IMEI or ICCID yet (the modem is Plan 10), so those contents send nothing, as the ESP32 does while the modem is down.
- **Console UART modes** (`websocketTarget` 1 or 2):
  - a MessageBuffer per port (2 KB), written only by `serial_rx[n]` through a subscriber (spec §6.4), drained by `console` into binary frames;
  - WebSocket messages go to `serial_write`;
  - a full buffer drops the frame and counts it.
- **`/api/status`:**
  - `ports[n].tx/rx` from the counters;
  - `mem.uart[n]` gets `overrun` and `dropped` (line errors, console drops, frames cut by overrun).

  The port `state` stays "NOT STARTED" until the bridge (Plan 08).
- **CubeMX (`mkioc.py`):**
  - USART1 and USART2 in the build (`MX_*_Init` stays uncalled; `uart_drv` calls `HAL_UART_Init` itself);
  - their DMA requests on DMA1 S2–S5;
  - their NVIC at 6, with the handlers not generated (ours replace them);
  - TIM2 global interrupt at 6.
- **Bench console (UART7):**
  - `T` + port + a hex Modbus request runs `serial_transact` and prints the answer, or that a marked frame was dropped;
  - `X` + port injects an XOFF, then an XON 1 s later, into that port's RX path during the next write (the fault injection of the bench).

## Rulings to approve

1. **TIM2 CC1/CC2 as the t3.5 one-shots,** not two more timers. Cost if wrong: TIM2's interrupt also runs for `now_us`'s compare work. It has none today.
2. **No XOFF sent by the gateway,** only honoured from the device.
3. **The RS232 path of UART1 is not tested** (no adapter on the bench). It shares TX and RX with RS485 (§3), so the RS485 tests cover the UART itself.
4. **`serial_hb` sends nothing for IMEI or ICCID contents** until Plan 10, as the ESP32 does while the modem is down.

## Bench (`test/bench/serial_check.py`)

As run: the board's two RS485 ports are wired to each other (A to A, B to B), and UART2 is UART1's counterpart, timed by the board itself (bench console `E`/`F`, `app/bench_serial.c`; finding S1). "The PC" below is that counterpart.


1. **Console UART mode, both ports:**
   - WebSocket → RS485 and RS485 → WebSocket;
   - 9600 8N1 and 230 400 8E1 (and 600 7O2 once);
   - a 1460-byte frame cut at `packetLength`;
   - frames cut by `packetInterval`.
2. **Modbus transaction** (`T`): the PC answers as a slave at **1200 8E1**: 11 bits a character, t1.5 = 13.75 ms, t3.5 ≈ 32.08 ms.
   - A good answer is returned.
   - An answer sent in two writes 20 ms apart is **dropped as a t1.5 violation, by the board's own count**. Windows' buffering does not prove a gap (Codex r1), so this case passes only when the board reports a violation and its counter goes up, not on a timeout.
   - Two writes 60 ms apart (beyond t3.5) are two frames, with no violation: the contrast.
   - After a violation, the next good answer is received (resync).
   - An answer from another slave id goes to the subscribers.
   - No answer times out.
   - **XOFF/XON** with `flowControl` 2. Two-wire RS485 lets one driver at a time on the bus (Modbus Serial Line §3.3.2), so the PC never sends while the gateway sends (Codex r2):
     - **on the wire:** the PC sends XOFF *first*, then a 2 KB write is asked from the WebSocket. Nothing arrives at the PC. The PC sends XON and the whole 2 KB arrives. XOFF and no XON: the write fails after its deadline, and the next write (after an XON) works;
     - **a pause in the middle of a running DMA**, by fault injection on the board: bench console `X` + port makes the RX path see an XOFF 20 ms into a 2 KB write, and an XON 1 s later. The PC sees the data stop, about 1 s of silence, then the rest: every byte, in order;
     - **not tested:** an XOFF received while TX runs, which needs a full-duplex line (RS232, no adapter on the bench).
3. **Continuous RX** at 230 400 for 60 s on UART1 while `net_check`'s UDP flood runs: 0 overrun, every byte counted (P2: bounded work, priorities).
4. **Line errors:** wrong parity from the PC; frames dropped and counted.
5. **DE:** half duplex works both ways at 230 400 with no lost first byte; the adapter sees no echo of its own frames.

Regressions: `web_check.py` (console cases), `store_check.py`.

## Bench findings

- **S1. The bench is the board's own two ports, not two USB-RS485 adapters.** The user wired UART1 and UART2 to each other. UART2 answers as the counterpart (`E<port>`: off, echo, a reply in two halves `gap_ms` apart, a stream, a send; `F<port>`: the frames received, with their lengths and gaps). The gaps on the wire are then the board's own: Windows' buffering could not prove a t1.5 gap (Codex r1), the board can. Cost: both ends are this firmware, so a fault common to both could hide; the line settings of the two ends differ in the parity case, which checks one against the other.
- **S2. A and B were crossed on the first wiring.** Nothing was framed; with RXINV set, the bytes came in right, which proved an inverted line. The user swapped the wires, then replaced two that carried nothing.
- **S3. DTCM overflowed (116 KB of 128) with the serial rings.** The DMA rings and TX buffers go to SRAM1 (DMA1 cannot reach DTCM; `check_elf.py` gates `s_uart_ring` and `s_uart_txbuf` there). The large buffers that no DMA uses moved from DTCM to AXI: the store's pool and chunk, the web's record buffer, the console's drain line and `bench_ota`'s buffer.
- **S4. The XON case must answer within the write's deadline.** The first `serial_check` sent the XON after about 2.2 s, when the write had already failed. It now sends it within 1 s; the case with no XON checks the deadline on its own.
- **S5. The second bench board's FlashDB held another firmware's layout** (4 KB sectors, Plan 06b B4): its Edge POST failed. The board had run firmware from outside this project. FlashDB is not patched (the user's rule). Instead, provisioning a board that ran another firmware wipes both chips:
  - `tools/flash.sh erase`: a mass erase of the internal flash and all of QSPI through the external loader; then `bind`, then `both`;
  - **console `Z`, then the line `WIPE`:** the app erases all of SPI4 (1024 sectors, 22 s) and resets. FlashDB formats every partition afresh (boot #1, edge generation 0), and the bootloader seeds the factory image from the app (`boot: install seeded-factory`).
  - **A trap:** after `flash.sh erase` and `both`, the bootloader installed an older 0.7.0 from SPI4's staging over the image just flashed (spec §11.2, as `flash.sh` notes). The fix is to upload the new image over UART7 (`board.py upload`), which replaces staging, before `Z WIPE`.
  - Cost if wrong: `Z` sits on the UART7 console next to `u` (upload) and `w` (stop the watchdog kicks), so whoever has that console can wipe the configs. The second line, `WIPE`, guards against a stray byte only.

## Code review round 1 (NOT APPROVED), and the fixes

1. **Events queued behind a busy reader** carried stale positions: a reader at 0 with events at 1024, 2048 and 3072 read bytes the DMA had already overwritten. `sf_ring_take` checks each event against the DMA's position *now*: a lap (or within 64 bytes of one) is an overrun, and the reader goes on from the DMA; an event behind the reader reads nothing. `test_serial_frame` cases.
2. **A transaction that ended in a violation** cleared the resync on its way back to transparent mode, so the tail could reach the subscribers. `sf_rx_mode` keeps a resync when leaving RTU (entering RTU still ends it, after the silence a transaction waits for), and `serial_transact` waits in RTU, at most 200 ms, for the silence after a broken answer. `test_serial_frame` case.
3. **Line errors came after the data:** the ISR posted the gap before the error, and `serial_rx` read the bytes before handling the error, so with `packetLength` 1 the byte in error went out. The ISR now posts the error first; `serial_rx` reads up to the byte before the error, handles the error, then reads the byte in error. A line error now drops its frame up to the next silence (the tail used to be a frame). `test_serial_frame` cases; on the bench the parity case with `packetLength` 1 passes before and after the fix, so it guards the regression only.
4. **Collision avoidance saw a busy bus as quiet:** `last_rx_us` moved only when `serial_rx` read (a DMA half or an RTO). `rx_quiet_since` also stamps it when the DMA's position moved or the USART is BUSY. The bench case failed first (the write went out mid-stream), then passed. On the way: `now_us() - rx_quiet_since(p)` evaluated `now_us()` first, which made the difference negative; the stamp is now taken first.
5. **The t3.5 one-shot** ignored the RTO's rounding to whole bits (at 1200 8E1: 17 bits, 14 167 µs, then 18 334 µs, 32 501 µs in all). `sf_t35_oneshot_us` is t3.5 less the RTO as programmed. `test_serial_frame` checks RTO + one-shot = t3.5 at 600 to 230 400 baud.
6. **`Z WIPE` against an OTA upload:** the store lock did not stop the web OTA writing staging. `flash_spi4_wipe` seals the chip under its (now recursive) lock, so every write through `flash_spi4()` fails from then on, then erases it. On the bench: `Z WIPE` during a web OTA upload; the upload is cut, and the next boot installs nothing (`seeded-factory` only). `Z` stays on the UART7 console (Codex: acceptable for provisioning).
7. **Minor:** `serial_check` checks that the LAN flood ran.

## Code review round 2 (NOT APPROVED), and the fixes

1. **The 200 ms bound on the tail wait** let a long tail end its resync on a transparent gap shorter than t3.5. No bound now: after a broken answer the port stays in RTU timing, with the bus released, and `serial_rx[n]` switches it back to transparent when t3.5 of silence ends the resync (`tail`, under `txn_lock`; a new transaction takes the mode back first). Bench case `tail`: failed before (3 bytes out), passes after.
2. **A byte in error could still go out** through a DMA half or TC posted before the USART's error, and the byte in error need not be the last one when the error interrupt runs late. Both interrupts now post a pending error first (`rx_errors`: the DMA moves a byte only after the USART set its flags). `sf_rx_ring`, which now holds `serial_rx`'s per-event reading, drops every byte since the last read on an error, with their frame, up to the next silence. `test_serial_frame` cases, including an error whose byte is not the last.
3. **A restart already set** (an OTA just done) would cut `Z WIPE` and leave staging to install. `sysmgr_restart_claim`: the first caller owns the one restart; `Z` claims it first and is refused when a restart is pending, and no restart asked for during the wipe happens. Bench: an OTA done, then `Z WIPE` at once: refused, and the restart of the OTA follows.

Regressions after round 2: `web_check.py` failed once on a connection timeout right after its 30 s silence case (the board kept running and answered ping), then passed 42 of 42 twice, once with the console recorded (no fault, no unexpected reset). `store_check.py` passes; slowest save 430 ms on the freshly formatted store.

## Code review round 3 (NOT APPROVED), and the fixes

1. **A transaction asked for inside a tail** waited only `gap_ms + 1` of quiet, then cleared `tail` and the resync: its request could go into a pause under t3.5. It now waits for `tail` to end first (within its timeout; else no answer). Bench (`tail`): the peer gets both requests whole, and the second starts 1267 ms after the first against 1234 ms before the fix, the extra 33 ms being t3.5 after the tail's end. The case passes before the fix too (a receiver is off while its port sends, so the collision does not show on this rig): it guards the regression.
2. **RTU timing was kept only after `SERIAL_DROPPED`:** a line error in a transaction that timed out went back to transparent, where a short gap ended its resync. The port now stays in RTU timing whenever a resync is on at the end of a transaction. Bench (`tail`, UART1 7E1 against UART2 8N1, so only UART1 sees parity errors): a line error, a tail past the 1 s timeout, a 20 ms pause, 2 more bytes. Before the fix the 2 bytes reached the subscribers; after it, nothing does.
