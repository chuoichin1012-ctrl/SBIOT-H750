# Plan 09d: 300 Edge points

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** the Edge model may hold **300 points** (the user, 2026-10-16, after Plan 09c's measurements), with no change to the flash layout and no factory reset on upgrade.

**Architecture:**
- **The body limit** for `edgeDevices` rises from 48 KB to **96 000 bytes**. A 300-point model as the UI sends it (minified, every field) takes about 68 KB, and up to about 90 KB with long names and formulas.
- **Records** grow from 8 KB to **16 000 bytes**, so one fills one 16 KB sector of the 256 KB edge DB. A save then holds the old generation (6 sectors) and the new one (6 sectors), and leaves 4 sectors for FlashDB's garbage collection. With 8 KB records, each still takes a whole sector, so 96 KB would not fit.
- **RAM:**
  - the store's record buffer (`s_chunk`) and the web's GET buffer (`s_record`) take 8 KB more AXI each;
  - the boot's temporary heap buffer grows to 96 000 + 1 bytes;
  - the model takes 216 B per point on the heap.
- **The report buffer keeps its 26 KB limit** (about 100 reported values): all 300 points are polled and serve linkage and remote access, but a report carries about 100 of them.

**Spec:** [2026-10-14-stm32-firmware-design.md](../specs/2026-10-14-stm32-firmware-design.md): §9.4 (limits, records), §5.3 (RAM), §10.3 (UI).

## Global Constraints

- **Keep it simple** (the user): no new mechanism. Run only the affected tests; TDD. Codex reviews before the PR.
- **No flash layout change:** the partitions and the 16 KB sectors stay. Records saved by earlier firmware (8 KB) stay readable.
- **The limits:**
  - 300 points;
  - 64 devices;
  - 5 Modbus TCP devices;
  - a body of 96 000 bytes;
  - a report buffer of 26 KB.

## Known limits (recorded, not handled)

- **A pinned generation takes room.** A model near 96 000 bytes takes 6 of the edge DB's 16 sectors, so two such generations fit (12 sectors), and the remaining 4 hold the manifest, the other small keys and FlashDB's garbage collection.
  - A GET pins the committed generation. A save made while the GET runs still commits, and the old generation stays until the GET ends.
  - A second save during that same GET would need a third generation. It finds no room and gets 500; the UI shows the error.
  - Once the GET has finished, saving again works.
  - With 8 KB records this happened too, only at a larger model size.
- **`slaves()` in `cfgmodel`** keeps `names[MAX_NODES + 64]` on the stack, 1.5 KB on the board at 300 points. It is reached only through `cfg_load("edgeDevices")`, and the board never loads `edgeDevices` that way: the model comes from the records, through `cfg_edge_load`. It is left as is.

3. **The boot with 300 points and the TLS sessions of 09c's scenario (c).** The heap keeps a margin. Pinned in T3 by `mem_check.py` with a 300-point model.

## Rulings to approve

1. `STORE_EDGE_RECORD` goes from 8192 to **16 000**, and `CFG_EDGE_MAX` and `WEB_EDGE_MAX` from 49 152 to **96 000** (6 records).
   - The store reads a record of any length up to `STORE_EDGE_RECORD`, so the 8 KB records of earlier generations still read, and the next save rewrites the model in 16 000-byte records.
2. `MAX_NODES` goes from 100 to 300 in `cfgmodel` and in the UI (`edge.ts`).
   - `cfgmodel`'s summary tables (`enode_t node[MAX_NODES]`, on the heap in the text walk) grow with it.
   - Nothing else in `edge_rt` is sized by points: the model, the index and the report buffer are allocated from the count.
3. **The UI checks the body's size** before it saves (`body(slaves)` within 96 000 bytes), and shows the error.
4. The report buffer stays at 26 KB; the UI's template check (`templateSize`) already enforces it.

## Measurements (T3, board)

`mem_check.py`, 300 points (120 on UART1, 100 on UART2, 5 Modbus TCP devices of 12, 2 IO, 18 virtual), a report of 100 values every 5 s over MQTT:

| Scenario | Heap free | Lowest | Largest block |
|---|---|---|---|
| (b) Edge alone | 199 600 | 117 192 | 140 464 |
| (c) + MQTT TLS, UART1 TLS client, UART2 HTTPS client | 133 984 | 102 888 | 104 600 |
| (d) + a 96 000-byte save during a GET | 135 472 | 102 344 | 104 600 |
| (e) 50 rounds of 96 000-byte save and GET, 4 MQTT reconnects | 133 704 | 66 784 | 114 024 |

- Plan 09c, with 100 points, measured (e) at 185 384 free, 142 240 lowest. The lowest is 75 KB less. The model accounts for about 43 KB of that (216 B for each of the 200 extra points); the rest was not broken down.
- The heap stays level within 1.7 KB over the 50 rounds.
- Every save returns 200, and every GET returns 200 with the bytes saved.
- MQTT reconnects 4 of 4 times, and 34 reports arrive in 191 s, each with 100 values.
- UART2 makes 176 HTTPS requests during the run.
- ADC 9.78 windows/s, late +0; the watchdog's longest gap is 200 ms.
- AXI: 490 688 of 524 288 B used; `s_chunk` and `s_record` take 7 808 B more each.
- The STORE_CUTS matrices on the host: the 96 000-byte save with garbage collection takes 430 operations, and a cut at each, plus 150 runs with 3 cuts, ends on one whole generation. A single write failure at each operation also ends consistent.

**Bench findings:**
- **A false flash timeout locked the store** (fixed). `spi_nor.c`'s `wait_ready` read the status register, then the clock. A task preempted between the two for over 6 ms (TLS handshakes of other tasks) took a busy status from before the preemption for a timeout. The FAL then locked the store until the next boot: Edge saves got 500, and the certificates could no longer be read, so TLS stopped.
  - The 300-point load made this show once, on a log write at 21 s.
  - The clock is now read before the status. `test_spi_nor` (fake HAL) failed with the old order.
  - The bootloader shares the file but polls without preemption, so it is not reflashed.
- **The first run used the old firmware.** SPI4 staging held an older image, which the bootloader installed over the SWD-flashed app at every boot (`flash.sh`'s note). Images now go over UART7 (`board.upload`).
  - Under the 300-point load with the old driver, one such upload stopped after its first block (probably the same timeout), and the image bytes ran as console commands. A factory reset cleared the load, and the upload then worked.
- **The `system` config left with only some of its fields came from `web_check`'s review case 1b.** It saved `{"hostname":…}` alone, and since Plan 09b a body replaces the whole config. Plan 09c's finding blamed an older bench run for this. Case 1b now saves the whole config it read.
- **`edge_check basic` read the status after closing its slaves,** so the points failed one by one before the read ("RUNNING 2/9"). The status is now read while the slaves still answer.

Other board runs: `edge_check basic remote memory` ok (heap lowest 108 808 with 300 points and MQTT on; every Edge task keeps over 2 KB of stack); `web_check` (the whole suite, the pinned generations on 2 records of 16 000 B) ok; `npm run test:pages` ok.
